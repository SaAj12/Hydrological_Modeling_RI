Station-wise analysis method used to generate Figure/Table analog outputs
=======================================================================

Output folder
-------------
All products were generated by:
  scripts/reproduce_collins_2021wr030480_stationwise.py

and written to:
  analysis_outputs/collins_2021wr030480_stationwise/

Data sources
------------
1) Discharge data (station metadata + time series):
   frontend/data/discharge_data.json

2) Climate index data:
   frontend/data/climate_indices_monthly.csv
   Columns used: AMO, NAO, PDO, PNA, ENSO (SOI), MEI, AO


Core event definition
---------------------
For each station:
1) Daily discharge values are parsed into a time series.
2) Annual maxima (AMS-like) are computed as the maximum daily value in each calendar year.
3) The station-level "Top-10 floods" are the 10 largest annual maxima over the full record.
4) The station-level "Top-2 floods" are the 2 largest annual maxima over the full record.

Note:
- This is a station-by-station analog workflow.
- The source paper performs many analyses at regional/CONUS pooled scales; here the same ideas are adapted per station.


Figure analog methods
---------------------
Figure 1 analog (fig1_station_<id>.png)
  - Uses AMS within the analysis period 2010-2025 only.
  - AMS is defined as the largest annual streamflow value in each year.
  - Top-10 and Top-2 floods are identified by ranking those AMS values within 2010-2025.
  - Plot shows AMS by year with visual highlighting for Top-10 and Top-2 events.

Figure 2 analog (fig2_station_<id>.png)
  - Bars: monthly relative frequencies (%) of Top-10 floods for that station.
  - Green dots: monthly relative frequencies (%) of Top-2 floods.
  - X-axis is in water-year month order: Oct, Nov, Dec, Jan, ..., Sep.
  - Uniform reference line at 1/12 and a 95% CI band are shown using a binomial approximation under a uniform-month null.

Figure 3 analog (fig3_station_<id>.png)
  - Monthly relative frequencies for:
    (a) all annual maxima (AMS),
    (b) Top-10 floods,
    (c) Top-2 floods.
  - Used for visual comparison of seasonality shape.

Figure 5 analog (fig5_station_<id>.png)
  - Hypergeometric expected distribution for the number of Top-10 events occurring in the analysis window 2010-2025.
  - Observed count in 2010-2025 is shown with a vertical marker.
  - N = available AMS years at station, K = number of Top-10 events, n = years in 2010-2025 with data.

  - This figure was previously produced, then removed by request.

Figure 7 analog (fig7_station_<id>.png)
  - Annual count series of Top-2 events (0/1 by year, aggregated at station-year).
  - Quasi-Poisson style trend fit (log link) over year.

Figure 8 analog (fig8_station_<id>.png)

Table analog methods
--------------------
Table 1 analog (table1_station_<id>.csv)
  - For Top-2 and Top-10 annual count series:
    - p-value for year-trend coefficient (quasi-Poisson style)
    - trend magnitude across station record
    - dispersion estimate (phi)

Table 2 analog (table2_station_<id>.csv)
  - For Top-2 and Top-10 counts, each climate index is tested separately:
    - quasi-Poisson style coefficient and p-value
    - trend-over-index-range metric
    - Pearson r^2 (%) between index and annual counts (when variance is non-constant)
  - Climate annual handling:
    - AMO/PDO/PNA/ENSO/MEI/AO: annual means from monthly values
    - NAO: winter (Dec-Mar) mean with one-year lag (nao_winter_lag1)
  - Analysis period for climate-index regressions is restricted to 2010-2025.
  - NAO analyses specifically use only nao_winter_lag1 (not same-year annual NAO).

Table 3 analog (table3_station_<id>.csv)
  - Seasonal composition table (DJF/MAM/JJA/SON fractions) for:
    - all AMS floods
    - Top-10 floods
    - Top-2 floods
  - This is a station-scale analog of seasonal attribution comparison.


Statistical/implementation notes
--------------------------------
1) Quasi-Poisson style fitting:
   - Poisson log-link model is fit by numerical optimization.
   - Overdispersion is estimated and used to inflate covariance (phi correction).
   - p-values are from normal approximation of corrected coefficient z-score.

2) Hypergeometric expectation:
   - Used for "expected occurrence in a target window" calculations.
   - Appropriate for finite-population draws without replacement.

3) CI in Figure 2:
   - One-sided 95% confidence intervals are computed from empirical monthly-frequency distributions generated
     under a non-seasonal circular-uniform model (equal month probability = 1/12).
   - Monte Carlo method:
     - simulate n floods across 12 months from the uniform model,
     - repeat 100,000 times,
     - compute monthly frequency distributions from the simulations,
     - use monthwise 5th and 95th percentiles as lower/upper one-sided 95% bounds.


Limitations and interpretation guidance
---------------------------------------
1) These outputs are "analogs" of the cited paper figures/tables, not exact reproductions of all paper procedures.
2) The paper's strongest inference is regional/CONUS pooled; station-wise analyses are less data-rich.
3) Using annual maxima only means sub-annual large events in the same year are not represented.
4) Some station/index regressions may be unstable with sparse counts; interpret p-values and coefficients cautiously.
5) Climate-index processing follows available local data and practical approximations.
